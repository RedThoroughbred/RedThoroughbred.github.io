01Start here

What SweepLab is and what you need

SweepLab helps you check a room you're staying in, such as a rental, a hotel room or a short stay, for hidden cameras. It doesn't decide whether something is a camera. It points you at the spots and devices worth a closer look, and you go and look.

  • An iPhone with iOS 18 or later. The lens and infrared checks use its flashlight and cameras.
  • A dark room: lights off, curtains closed. Both light checks work far better in the dark.
  • For the infrared check's self-test, a TV remote of the classic infrared kind (many streaming remotes use Bluetooth instead).
  • Optional: a SweepLab Puck for the Wi-Fi radar. Everything else works without one. See the puck guide.
  • No account. Nothing leaves your iPhone.
  1. Turn the lights off and close the curtains.
  2. Open SweepLab and tap Start the room check.
  3. Follow each step. Skip any you can't do.
  4. Go through What to look at with your own flashlight.

02Home

Check this room

Four short checks, about seven minutes

The home screen leads with the guided check. Its card shows the four steps (Lens, Infrared, Wi-Fi, By hand) and which are done, skipped or next. The button reads Start the room check, then Continue the room check, then See what to look at once all four are done. New room forgets this room and starts over.

Below it, Or run one check has a tile for each check on its own, with its state: the light mode for the lens scan, which camera the infrared check will use and whether it has been tested, the puck's state, and how many places you've ticked off by hand. When anything has been flagged, a line such as 2 things to look at so far takes you to the results.

At the bottom: the puck's connection and battery (when it reports one), and What this can and can't find.

03The room check

Four checks, in order

Why, what to do, skip, redo

The guided check opens the current step with Why (one sentence on why the check works) and What to do (three things, in order). One button at the bottom starts the step. Finished steps fold up to what they found, such as nothing flagged or 2 spots to check, with a Redo button.

1 · Lens glint scan
About 2 minutes. Lights off, curtains closed. Sweep the room with the flashlight. See Lens glint scan.
2 · Infrared check
About 1 minute. Pan the room with the camera SweepLab recommends. See Infrared check.
3 · Wi-Fi radar
About 2 minutes. Let the puck listen, then run the motion test. No puck? Tap I don't have a puck, skip. See Wi-Fi radar.
4 · Look by hand
About 2 minutes. Tick off the usual hiding places, then tap I've looked: show the results. See Look by hand.

Skip this check moves on without running a step; the results will show it as skipped, so a quiet result is never mistaken for a full check. Start over clears the spots found so far and the checklist.

04Lens glint scan

Sweep with the flashlight

Strobe or steady light, sweep coaching, rings

A camera lens bounces light straight back toward where it came from. In a dark room, the flashlight makes a lens show up as a tiny, sharp, bright point, even through a pinhole. The bounce only comes back when the phone is roughly in front of the lens (inside the camera's own view) and within about 2–3 m, which is as far as the iPhone's flashlight reaches.

Strobe or steady

The first time, SweepLab explains that the strobe scan switches the flashlight on and off several times a second, and asks you to choose:

  • Use flashing light: SweepLab compares frames with and without the flash. A point that lights up only with the flash is worth a look; a lamp or an LED stays lit either way. This makes lens reflections much easier to tell apart from shiny surfaces.
  • Use steady light: the flashlight stays on. It works the same way, with a little less certainty.

Flashing light. If flashing lights affect you or anyone in the room, choose Use steady light.

Doing the scan

  1. Turn the lights off and close the curtains. Hold the phone at eye level.
  2. When a scan starts, SweepLab may show Measuring the flashlight: point at a wall or furniture 0.5–2 m away and hold still for two seconds.
  3. Stand where a camera would want to look: the bed, the shower, the desk.
  4. Sweep slowly across every wall, shelf and object, about a hand-width a second, within 2–3 m of what you're checking. Then do it again from a second spot.
  5. Tap Done.

The pill at the top coaches your pace: Sweep slowly, Good pace, or Slow down when you're moving too fast for a lens to flash back. Tips at the bottom rotate through where to stand and what to look at.

A ring marks each spot. Tap a ring to zoom in on it. The bottom bar counts Spots found in this scan and how many are In view. If the puck is connected, a small Wi-Fi chip in the top bar shows how many devices are worth a look; tap it to open the radar without leaving the scan.

What the spots mean

Lit up only with the flash
Strobe light. A small point that lit up only with the flash: could be a camera lens. Look closely at that spot from where you stood, or photograph it.
Bright pinpoint that stayed put
Steady light. A small, sharp point that stayed in place while you moved: could be a lens, a small light or a shiny screw. Switch your flashlight off: a lens goes dark, an LED does not.

Shiny screws, glass edges, glossy plastic, water drops and tiny indicator lights glint too. A ring is a spot to check by hand, not proof of a camera.

On iPhones with LiDAR, Home also shows an experimental LiDAR lens test under More checks. Whether a lens shows up in the iPhone's depth picture is unproven, and nothing from that test goes into your results.

05Infrared check

Look for night-vision lights

The TV-remote test, then a slow pan

Many cameras light a dark room with infrared LEDs your eyes can't see. Some iPhone cameras can see them, as small purple or white dots, and some can't, because of the filter in front of the sensor. Front and rear cameras filter differently, which is why SweepLab tests them.

The TV-remote test

The first time, the check starts with a quick self-test (you can run it again from the Remote test button):

  1. Get a TV remote ready. Hold it 10–30 cm from the camera being tested (for the front camera, the top edge of the screen), pointing at it. Don't press yet.
  2. Press and hold any button now. Use a classic infrared remote: many streaming remotes use Bluetooth instead.
  3. If SweepLab says This camera sees infrared, tap Start the IR check. If it says This camera didn't see the remote, tap Try the next camera, or Test again.

SweepLab remembers the answer for each camera and uses one that sees infrared. The camera button at the top switches cameras by hand.

If none of your iPhone's cameras sees the remote, they filter infrared too strongly for this check. Rely on the lens glint scan instead, or try another remote.

The check

  1. Keep the lights off.
  2. Pan slowly around the room. With the front camera, turn the screen toward the room.
  3. Violet rings mark small, steady lights. If you can't see one with your own eyes, it may be infrared.
  4. Tap Done.

Only pinpoints alone in the dark count: highlights on paper, a wall or a shiny box are ignored, and the same light seen again as you pan is folded into one finding, marked as seen more than once. Tap the moon at the top to dim the screen so it doesn't light the room; rings still appear and you feel a tap.

Steady light the camera sees
A small light the camera sees in the dark. If you can't see it with your own eyes, it may be an infrared LED of the kind night-vision cameras use. Look closely at what it's part of.

The infrared check only finds a camera that is switched on with its night-vision lights lit.

06Wi-Fi radar

Let the puck listen

Needs the SweepLab Puck

A camera streaming video uploads steadily, all the time. The SweepLab Puck listens to every Wi-Fi device in range, on any network, including ones you can't join. It can't see what devices send, only how much and how steadily, and the app ranks them by how much they upload like a camera. The puck only listens: it never joins a network and never transmits on Wi-Fi.

  1. Power the puck from your iPhone's USB-C port, a charger or a battery pack, and put it on a table somewhere in the middle of the room. See powering the puck.
  2. Open Wi-Fi radar (the third tile, or step 3 of the room check). The first time, How to use the puck explains it; tap Connect the puck and allow Bluetooth.
  3. When the card says the puck is connected, tap Listen and give it a minute. The puck visits each Wi-Fi channel in turn.
  4. Run the motion test.
  5. In the room check, tap Done here: continue the room check.

The pill at the top right shows the link: Listening, Paused, Searching, Connecting, Reconnecting, Bluetooth off or Off. Pause stops listening. While it listens, a line may say it's focusing on a few busy channels to measure their uploads properly.

Blink the puck
Flashes the puck's light for a few seconds, to find it.
More → Listen on
2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, or both (the default).
More → New session

07The signal ladder

Reading Heard nearby

Rows, left to right, dot size, scores

Every device the puck hears is a dot on a ladder. The headline sums it up, for example 8 devices, 2 worth a look, or none uploading like a camera.

Rows: how strong the signal is

A strong signal usually means close. The puck has one antenna, so it can't tell which direction a device is in, only roughly how far. Walls, mirrors and metal weaken signals, so a camera behind a mirror can read as "nearby".

Very close −50 dBm and upStrong signal: probably within a few steps of the puck.
In the room −51 to −64Good signal: probably this room.
Nearby −65 to −78Weaker signal: the next room, or through a wall.
Far below −78Faint signal: far away or well shielded.
Signal unknown via routerHeard only through the router: the puck hears the router confirming the device's data but never the device itself, which is common on fast 5 GHz networks. The upload is real; the distance isn't known. It moves onto a row as soon as the puck hears the device directly.

The ranges are rough. Indoors, walls move them.

Left to right: how much it uploads like a camera

Each device gets a score from 0 to 100: 0 is quiet; 100 is a constant stream. A device scoring 50 or more is Worth a look; its dot pulses and it goes into your results.

0–24 · Quiet
No sign of a video stream.
25–49 · Some uplink
Sends a little now and then, like most connected gadgets.
50–74 · Worth a look
Uploads often enough to be worth a look.
75–100 · Streams like a camera
Uploads steadily and continuously, the pattern of a live video stream. Still not a verdict: a TV streaming looks the same.

Dot size and bubbles

A bigger dot is uploading more right now; its colour follows the score. When several devices land too close together to draw apart, they fold into one bubble: ×7 means seven devices. Tap it to open it up.

Full screen

Tap the expand button to fill the screen. Pinch to zoom, drag to move, double-tap to zoom in and back out; names show up as you zoom in. Filter by All, Worth a look, 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz, and tap Fit to see everything again. The faint line behind a dot is where its score has been over the last half minute.

Tap a dot to see its signal, upload, score and how long it's been heard, then Details, or This is mine for your own phone, laptop or TV: it dims the dot and leaves it out of "worth a look", for this scan only.

08One device

Find it, then decide

The device sheet

Tap a device, in the list under Ranked by upload or on the ladder, to open its sheet.

  • The name is the device's maker, read from the first part of its Wi-Fi address (the maker prefix), or Private address when the address is randomized, as phones, tablets and laptops do for privacy. Unknown maker means the prefix isn't one the app knows. The maker is a hint, never proof.
  • Tags: sells cameras (the maker sells Wi-Fi cameras, among other things), Wi-Fi module (the maker builds the Wi-Fi chips inside many cheap cameras, plugs and gadgets), phone/laptop (a randomized address), mine.
  • Find it shows the live signal in dBm with warmer, colder or steady. Carry the puck slowly around the room: the number climbs toward −30 as you get close. Check anything with a pinhole, a lens, or a power cable it doesn't need. For a device with an unknown signal, move the puck around until a number appears.
  • Upload, last minute: the rate now, how steady it is, how long it's been heard, and the band.
  • Motion test: its upload with you moving and with the room empty, once you've run the test.
  • What this means, in plain words, ending with the reminder that a TV streaming, a laptop backing up or a phone on a video call look the same to the puck.

09The motion test

Does anything react to you?

15 s moving · 8 s to leave · 15 s away

A camera's upload shrinks when the room it watches goes empty and still. The motion test compares each device's upload with you moving in the room and with the room empty. Tap Listen first so the puck has devices to compare, then Start the motion test.

  1. Walk around the room (15 seconds, Keep moving). Move like you normally would: walk, wave, sit down and stand up. A camera that sees you sends more data.
  2. Step out and close the door (8 seconds, Leave now). Take the phone or leave it on a table; either works. The room needs to be empty and still.
  3. Stay outside (15 seconds, Stay out). The puck is measuring the empty room. Your phone buzzes when you can come back in.

The result lists the devices with their upload both ways, such as 1.2 Mbps moving → 210 kbps away, −82%. Devices whose upload dropped a lot are marked reacted to you and go to the top of your results. Nothing reacted to you lowers the odds; it can't rule a camera out. Run it again any time; Reset clears it.

10Look by hand

Nothing beats looking

The checklist

The usual hiding places are the ones with a view of the bed or the shower and a reason to have power. Look closely at each with your own flashlight and tick it off. Look for pinholes, odd lenses, and gadgets facing the bed or shower, and check anything the scans flagged, up close.

Bathroom
Start here. Shower area, towel hooks, the mirror frame and anything facing the toilet or shower.
Bedroom
Start here. Everything with a view of the bed: shelves, headboard, wall art, the TV.
Smoke detectors
The classic hiding place. Look for a pinhole facing the bed or shower.
Clocks and radios
Alarm clocks and speakers by the bed often face it directly.
Chargers and USB plugs
Wall chargers and power strips that seem out of place, or that face the room.
Vents and air purifiers
Grilles and slots high on the wall or ceiling.
TV area
The TV, set-top boxes, soundbars and anything on the shelf around them.

11Results

What to look at

One ranked list from every check

Results gather everything the checks flagged into one list of things to look at by hand, most urgent first. They never say "camera found".

  • The summary: N things to look at, or Nothing was flagged. Clear doesn't mean safe: it lowers the odds; it can't rule a camera out.
  • Covered so far: each check and what it found (2 spots to check, nothing flagged, 2 worth a look, 4 of 7 places), or skipped / not run.
  • Look at these, in order: lens spots and infrared lights with a snapshot of where they were (tap it to see it larger, with a ring where the spot was) and a strength from 0 to 100; Wi-Fi devices worth a look, tagged very close, reacted to you or maker sells cameras. The strongest eight show first; Show N weaker shows the rest. Tap a Wi-Fi device to open its sheet.
  • When you've looked at a spot, tap I looked: not a camera. It moves to You checked these (tap again to undo).
  • Still to look at by hand: checklist places you haven't ticked, with Open checklist.
  • If you find a camera and What these checks can miss.

Clear (top right) clears the spots found so far. Results are kept only while the app is open: closing SweepLab clears them.

12If you find a camera

What to do

  1. Don't touch it, unplug it or take it apart. Cover it with a towel if you need privacy right now.
  2. Photograph it where it is: close up, and from across the room so the position is clear.
  3. Report it to the booking platform or the hotel's front desk, and to the local police. Keep your booking details.
  4. If you feel unsafe, leave. Ask the platform or the hotel to move you; many booking platforms have a 24-hour safety line.

13What it can and can't find

Limits, honestly

None of these checks can promise a room is clear. Each catches some cameras and misses others, so run all four and look by hand. The same text is in the app under What this can and can't find.

What it can miss

  • A lens only flashes back when you're nearly in line with it, so sweep from several spots. Pinholes behind dark glass and anything beyond the flashlight's reach (about 2–3 m) can be missed.
  • A camera that is switched off only shows up in the lens scan. The infrared check needs it on with night-vision lights lit, and the Wi-Fi radar needs it streaming.
  • The Wi-Fi radar can miss a camera whose router is out of the puck's range (it only counts what it hears at roughly −80 dBm or stronger), and newer Wi-Fi 6 routers that schedule several devices' uploads together can hide one device's stream inside the group.
  • It can't hear cameras that record to a memory card without streaming, cameras on a cellular SIM card, or wired cameras. The lens and infrared checks are the backstop for those.
  • Devices on fast 5 GHz networks may show as signal unknown until the puck hears them directly.
  • A clear result does not mean a room has no cameras.

What it can mistake for a camera

  • Lens scan: shiny screws, glass edges, glossy plastic, water drops and tiny indicator lights. That's why results are spots to check by hand.
  • Wi-Fi radar: a TV streaming, a laptop backing up, a phone on a video call, a smart speaker: anything that uploads steadily. A high score is a device to go and find, and the motion test is how you tell them apart.

14Troubleshooting

When something doesn't work

Can't use the camera
SweepLab needs the camera for the lens and infrared checks. Go to Settings → SweepLab and turn on Camera.
Measuring the flashlight
Point at a wall or furniture 0.5–2 m away and hold still for two seconds.
Lots of rings
Shiny things glint. Slow down, sweep from a second spot, and check the strongest first. Switching your own flashlight off tells a lens (goes dark) from an LED (stays lit).
Remote not seen
Use a classic infrared remote and hold a button down 10–30 cm from the camera being tested. Try the next camera. If none sees it, rely on the lens scan.
Puck not found
Is it powered, with its light on? Keep it within a few metres of your iPhone. Check that Bluetooth is on, and that Settings → SweepLab → Bluetooth is allowed; if Bluetooth isn't available, the app offers Open Settings. See the puck guide.
Puck out of range
The app waits for it and reconnects on its own. Is it still powered?
Nothing on the radar
Tap Listen and give it a minute: the puck visits each channel in turn.
Motion test greyed out
Tap Listen first so the puck has devices to compare.
Signal unknown
The device is heard only through the router, which is common on fast 5 GHz networks. Move the puck around the room; a signal appears as soon as the puck hears the device directly.
My own devices score high
Your phone, laptop or TV may upload steadily too. Tap the dot and choose This is mine to dim it for this scan.
Results are gone
Results are kept only while the app is open. Run the checks again.
