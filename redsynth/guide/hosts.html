GarageBand and MIDI

RedSynth Pad is two things in one purchase: an app you play by itself, and an Audio Unit, a plugin that loads inside music apps. Same engine, same sounds, same photo.

First: open the app once

The plugin lives inside the app. Opening RedSynth Pad once tells your iPad or Mac that the plugin exists. After that, music apps can find it. If a music app was already open, quit it and open it again.

Music apps list it as RedSynth Pad, made by RedSynth.

GarageBand on a Mac

  1. Create a project and add a Software Instrument track.
  2. Open Smart Controls (press B).
  3. In the plug-in area on the left, click the instrument slot. It shows the current instrument's name.
  4. Choose AU Instruments → RedSynth → RedSynth Pad.
  5. Click the plugin's name to open its window. Play.

If you don't see AU Instruments, look in GarageBand → Settings → Audio/MIDI for Enable Audio Units and tick it.

GarageBand on an iPad

  1. Create a song and choose the Keyboard.
  2. Tap the instrument's name at the top.
  3. Under Audio Unit Extensions, choose RedSynth Pad.
  4. The plugin's own view opens from the same place. Tap its Pad button to play the photo by touch, right inside GarageBand.

Other music apps

RedSynth Pad is a standard AUv3 instrument, so other apps that host Audio Unit instruments, on Mac and iPad, should be able to load it. Look for it in the app's list of Audio Units or AUv3 instruments. It passes Apple's Audio Unit validation tool. So far it has been checked in GarageBand on the Mac; other hosts have not been tried yet.

Apps that only load VST or CLAP plugins can't load it.

The plugin's panel

It's the same instrument as the app, arranged to fit. In a small panel the buttons become icons. Make the window bigger and you get the full layout.

  • Pad swaps the photo for the pad.
  • Photo, Sounds, Help, Guide and Play do what they do in the app.
  • There's no Edit button in the plugin. Your music app's parameter list shows every control instead (below).
  • You can drop a photo on it on a Mac.

What's saved with your song

  • The sound, every control as you left it.
  • The photo, or the two or three photos of a space. They're stored inside the project, so the song opens with its instrument even on another device with RedSynth Pad installed.

Reopening a project doesn't replay the breakdown. The instrument is simply there.

The app's built-in sounds also appear as the plugin's presets, and your music app's own preset saving works too.

Automation: record a knob's movement

Everything you can turn is a parameter your music app can see, grouped the way the reference groups them. The most useful to record:

  • The four macros: Tone, Space, Grit, Wobble.
  • Scan: the scan line travelling through the photo. Scan Direction turns it: Down, Across or Diagonal.
  • The space's loop: its place, size and drift.
  • Cutoff, and any effect's mix.

In GarageBand, use the track's automation view. Other music apps have automation lanes of their own: choose the parameter from the plugin's list, or record while you turn the knob.

Why these are parameters and not just MIDI: GarageBand on iPad passes very little MIDI control data to plugins. So the macros, the scan line and the Touch FX effects are real parameters, and they work everywhere.

Tempo

Inside a music app, RedSynth Pad follows the song's tempo. Synced delay, LFOs, the Touch FX delay and gate, sample loops and your own loops all keep the beat.

One limit today: loops launch on RedSynth Pad's own bar line, which may not be the song's bar line. Start them by ear, or record their output.

MIDI keyboards and controllers

In the app, any MIDI controller your device sees just works: plug it in, or pair it over Bluetooth in your device's settings. Nothing to set up. In a music app, the music app sends the notes.

From the controllerWhat it does
KeysPlay the sound. How hard you hit sets loudness and brightness.
Pitch wheelBends the note (Bend Range sets how far).
Mod wheelOpens the filter.
Sustain pedalHolds notes.
Channel 10Plays the drum kit, on the standard drum notes.
Channel 11, notes 36 to 51Plays the sixteen sample slots.
MPE controllersEach finger gets its own bend, slide and pressure when MPE Zone is on.

Everything is also reachable by touch. You never need a controller.

Limits inside a music app

  • No microphone. Apple doesn't let plugins record. Record samples in the app.
  • GarageBand may send everything on one channel, so the drum kit and the samples might not be reachable from GarageBand's own keyboard. The plugin's own pad plays them: use its Drums and Samples modes.
  • Several copies share memory. On an iPad, all the copies of a plugin in one song share a fixed amount of memory set by the system. If a song with many copies misbehaves, freeze or bounce some tracks.
