Synth basics: what the knobs mean

Ten ideas, one at a time, each with a drawing and a sound. Read them in order and every knob in Edit will make sense.

The sounds are RedSynth's own engine, the one in the app, rendered on a computer. Each starts from a plain sound (mostly one oscillator, filter open) and changes only what it shows. Their loudness is evened out so you can compare them. Headphones help for the low and the wide ones.

1. A sound is a wave

A sound is air moving back and forth. Draw that movement and you get a wave.

  • Pitch is how fast it repeats. Twice as fast is one octave higher.
  • Loudness is how tall it is. A taller wave pushes more air.

C3 repeats 131 times a second. C5 repeats 523 times: four times as fast, so two octaves up. In the second clip the note is the same; only the wave's height changes.

2. The four wave shapes

An oscillator makes the raw wave. Its shape decides the tone. Every shape is a mix of harmonics: the note itself, plus quieter copies at 2, 3, 4… times its speed.

More harmonics sound brighter. The bars show the first twelve of each shape.

Measured on these four clips, the average height of the sound (its spectral centroid) is 131 Hz for the sine, 327 Hz for the triangle, 2,940 Hz for the square and 3,290 Hz for the saw. Brighter, by the numbers.

In the app: Osc 1 Wave and Osc 2 Wave, in the order Saw, Square, Triangle, Sine · see them in Every knob

3. The filter

A filter takes harmonics away. Start bright, with a saw, and filter it darker. Three kinds you'll meet:

RedSynth's filter is a low-pass. High-pass and band-pass are here so the words make sense when you meet them in other synths.

  • Cutoff is where the cutting starts. Low is dark; high is bright.
  • Resonance is a peak right at the cutoff. A little adds focus. A lot whistles.

Two kinds of low-pass, chosen by Filter Type:

  • SVF: clean and precise. It cuts 12 dB per octave.
  • Ladder: a transistor ladder like classic analog synths. Steeper (24 dB per octave), warmer, and it growls when you push Drive.

Key Track lets high notes open the filter more, like a real instrument.

In the app: the Filter card on the Sound page · see them in Every knob

4. Envelopes: ADSR

An envelope shapes each note in time, from the moment you press the key to after you let go. It has four parts.

  • Attack: how long it takes to rise. Short is a hit; long is a swell.
  • Decay: how long it takes to fall to the held level.
  • Sustain: the held level, while the key is down. A level, not a time.
  • Release: how long it fades after you let go.

There are two envelopes. The amp envelope shapes loudness. The filter envelope shapes brightness: it opens the cutoff, as far as Filter Env Amount says, then lets it close.

In the app: the Filter envelope and Amp envelope cards · see them in Every knob

5. LFO: a slow hand on a knob

LFO means low-frequency oscillator. It is a wave too, but slow, from 40 times a second down to once every 50 seconds. Usually too slow to hear as a note. Instead, it turns a knob back and forth for you.

  • Rate: how fast it turns the knob.
  • Route: which knob it turns. Each LFO has three routes.
  • Route Amount: how far. Minus turns it the other way. On pitch, 100 % is a whole octave each way, so a vibrato needs only a few percent.

PitchVibrato: the note wavers up and down.

The six LFO shapes

  • Sync: on, the LFO locks to the tempo, and its length is a note: from a dotted whole note to a 1/32 triplet. Off, Rate is in hertz (times a second).
  • Trigger: Free runs one wave for every note, so a chord wobbles together. Key starts it afresh with each note.
  • Fade In: the movement starts gently and builds, like a singer's late vibrato.

In the app: the LFO 1 and LFO 2 cards on the Motion page · see them in Every knob

6. Two oscillators, sub, unison

Two oscillators at exactly the same pitch just sound louder. Tune one a few cents away (100 cents is a semitone) and their peaks drift in and out of line. The sound swells slowly and feels wider. That's detune.

  • Osc 2 Coarse moves Osc 2 in semitones: +7 is a fifth up, −12 an octave down. Fine moves it in cents.
  • Sub: a third oscillator one octave below Osc 1, square or sine. Weight you feel more than hear.
  • Unison: up to seven copies of each oscillator, detuned from each other (Unison Detune) and spread left and right (Unison Spread). Thick and wide.

In the app: the Oscillator 1, Oscillator 2 and Sources cards · see them in Every knob

10. Where the photo comes in

A photo plays through this same synth. In Scan, Osc 1 plays a row of your photo as its wave: a smooth row is a soft wave, a busy row a jagged one, brighter and buzzier, like a saw. When the photo comes in, it also sets the filter, envelopes and effects around it, once. Strike and Space go further: the photo becomes an object you hit, and it rings. Read How a photo becomes a sound, or watch it step by step in How it works.

In the app: the Photo page · see them in Every knob
