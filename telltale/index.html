Telltale

Check the room before you unpack.

For rentals, hotels and short stays. Telltale walks you through five checks in under ten minutes, with only your iPhone: a flashlight lens scan, an infrared check, a lights-off test, a listen-only Wi-Fi check and a tracker check. It can't promise a room is clear. It shows you the telltale signs and where to look.

01What makes it different

It shows its work

Many camera apps put their results behind a weekly plan. Telltale shows you what it saw and why, and lets you decide.

Lights-off test

What switches on in the dark

Prop the phone up and switch the light off. Night-vision cameras turn their infrared lights on within seconds, and Telltale rings what came on after the dark arrived.

Flash-only rings

Points that blink with the flash

The flashlight pulses and Telltale compares frames with and without it. A lens lights up only with the flash; a lamp or a standby LED stays lit either way.

Swept strip

Where you've already looked

A strip fills in as you turn and tilt, so you can see which directions of the room you've swept and which you haven't.

Listen-only Wi-Fi

Never touches their devices

The Wi-Fi check only hears what devices announce about themselves. It never connects to, logs in to or probes anything on someone else's network.

02How it works

Five checks, under ten minutes

  1. Lights off, curtains closed. Open Telltale and tap Start the room check. Each step says what to do, why it works, and what you found.
  2. Sweep with the flashlight, then look for infrared. The lens scan looks for the tiny point a lens sends back. The infrared check looks for night-vision lights, and the lights-off test watches what comes on in the dark.
  3. Listen to the Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. Join the rental's Wi-Fi and hear what its devices say they are. Then walk around with your bags while Telltale listens for item trackers.
  4. Look by hand. You get one list of things to look at, most urgent first, plus the usual hiding places. You go and look, and you decide what each one is.

03Lens glint scan

The flashlight finds what a lens sends back

A camera lens bounces light straight back toward where it came from, even through a pinhole. With the lights off, the flashlight makes a lens show up as a tiny, sharp, bright point.

  • Flash-only rings. The flashlight blinks several times a second and Telltale compares frames with and without it. A point that lights up only with the flash gets a ring.
  • Steady light if flashing lights affect you or anyone in the room. Telltale asks before the first pulse.
  • Sweep coaching and the swept strip. A lens only flashes back from roughly in front of it and within about 2–3 m. The app tells you when you're moving too fast, and the strip shows which directions you've covered.
  • Shiny screws, glass edges and water drops glint too, so every ring is a spot to check by hand, never a verdict.

04Infrared check and the lights-off test

Night-vision lights your eyes can't see

Many cameras light a dark room with infrared LEDs. Some iPhone cameras can see that light, and Telltale helps you find one that does.

  • The TV-remote test. Point a classic infrared TV remote at the camera and press a button. If the camera sees the remote's light, it can see infrared. If not, Telltale suggests another camera.
  • Pan slowly with the lights off. Violet rings mark small, steady lights. If you can't see one with your own eyes, it may be infrared.
  • The lights-off test. Prop the phone facing a wall with the light on, then switch the light off. A night-vision camera's light sensor turns its infrared LEDs on within seconds. Telltale watches for 15 seconds and rings only the lights that came on after the dark; anything already glowing is set aside.
  • Tap the moon to dim the screen so it doesn't light the room. Rings still appear, and you feel a tap.

A night light on a timer or a charger's LED can come on in the dark too. A ring is a place to look, not a verdict.

05Network check

Hear what the Wi-Fi says it is

Many Wi-Fi cameras announce themselves on the network they're on, by name or as a video service. Join the rental's Wi-Fi and Telltale listens for about 25 seconds.

  • Camera-like devices describe themselves as a camera or a video stream. Maybes have weaker hints, like a maker that also sells cameras. Each one says why it's on the list.
  • It only listens. Telltale never connects to a device, logs in, opens a stream or scans its ports. Probing devices on someone else's network isn't something a guest should do.
  • Camera-like means the device is on this Wi-Fi, not that it's in your room. Doorbells and outdoor cameras are common.

06Tracker check

Trackers that stay close to you

Item trackers send short Bluetooth signals all the time. Walk around with your bags for about a minute while Telltale listens.

  • It hears Tile, Samsung SmartTag, Chipolo, Pebblebee and Find Hub tags, and tags that use the industry's unwanted-tracking signal.
  • A tag is worth a look when it says it has been away from its owner, or when it stays close the whole time you move.
  • It can't hear AirTags. iOS keeps those to itself and warns you about an unknown AirTag on its own.
  • It only listens: it never connects to a tag, rings it or changes it, and it stops when you leave the app.

07Results

One list of what to look at

Results never say "camera found" or "all clear". They say what was seen, where, and what to do about it.

  • The ring shows which checks ran and what each found, so a quiet result is never mistaken for a clear room.
  • Every lens or infrared spot keeps a snapshot of where it was. Go and look, then mark it as looked at.
  • Save a PDF report with the time, what was checked and what was flagged, to keep or to send to the host or the booking platform.
  • If you find a camera: don't touch it, photograph it where it is, report it to the booking platform or the front desk and to the local police, and leave if you feel unsafe. More in the guide.

08Honest limits

What it can and can't do

None of these checks can promise a room is clear. Each catches some cameras and misses others, so Telltale runs five and sends you to look by hand.

The light checks

Need the right angle

A lens only flashes back when you're nearly in line with it and within about 2–3 m. Pinholes behind dark glass can be missed. A camera that's switched off only shows up in the lens scan.

Infrared

Depends on your iPhone

Some iPhone cameras filter infrared too strongly to see it. The TV-remote test tells you which of yours can; if none can, the lens scan is your backstop.

The Wi-Fi check

Hears only what's announced

It hears devices on the Wi-Fi you joined that announce themselves. Cameras that record to a memory card, use a cellular SIM, are wired or stay quiet won't show up.

A clear result

Isn't proof

Nothing flagged lowers the odds; it doesn't mean a room has no cameras. That's why the last step is always looking by hand. The full list

10FAQ

Common questions

Will Telltale find every hidden camera?

No, and nothing can promise that. Telltale gives you five ways to check and a list of things to look at. Cameras that are switched off, out of the flashlight's reach, wired, on a cellular SIM or recording to a memory card can be missed by some or all of the checks. A clear result lowers the odds; it isn't proof.

Why one price instead of a subscription?

You check a room a few times a year. Paying every week for that didn't feel right, and there's no server behind the app to pay for: everything runs on your iPhone.

Does the Wi-Fi check connect to the host's devices?

No. It only listens to what devices on the Wi-Fi you joined announce about themselves. It never connects to them, logs in, opens a video stream or scans their ports.

Can it find AirTags?

No. iOS doesn't let apps hear AirTags. Your iPhone warns you on its own when an unknown AirTag travels with you. Telltale's tracker check covers Tile, SmartTag, Chipolo, Pebblebee and Find Hub tags.

Which iPhones work?

Any iPhone with iOS 18 or later. Whether the infrared check works depends on your iPhone's cameras: the TV-remote test tells you.

Is anything uploaded or saved?

No. There's no account and nothing is uploaded. Camera frames are analysed on the phone as you scan. A PDF report is saved only when you ask for one, and it stays on your phone unless you share it. See the privacy policy.

What's next?

An optional Wi-Fi listener you can build yourself from a small off-the-shelf board, with open-source firmware. It hears Wi-Fi devices that upload steadily like a streaming camera, even on networks you can't join. It comes in a later update, and this page will say when.

When can I get it?
