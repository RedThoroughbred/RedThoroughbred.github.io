SweepLab

A room check for hidden cameras, on your iPhone.

For rentals, hotels and short stays. Four short checks walk you around the room: a flashlight lens scan, an infrared check, an optional Wi-Fi radar with the SweepLab Puck, and a list of places to look by hand. SweepLab can't promise a room is clear. It tells you where to look.

01How it works

Four steps, about seven minutes

  1. Lights off, curtains closed. Open SweepLab and tap Start the room check. It walks you through each check: what to do, why it works, and what you found.
  2. Sweep with the flashlight, then pan for infrared. The lens scan looks for the tiny bright point a camera lens sends back. The infrared check looks for night-vision lights your eyes can't see.
  3. Let the puck listen. Optional. Leave the SweepLab Puck in the room, tap Listen, then run the motion test: walk around, step out, and see whose upload dropped.
  4. Look by hand. You get one list of things to look at, most urgent first, plus the usual hiding places. You go and look, and you decide what each one is.

03Lens glint scan

The flashlight finds what a lens sends back

A camera lens bounces light straight back toward where it came from, even through a pinhole. With the lights off, the flashlight makes a lens show up as a tiny, sharp, bright point.

  • Strobe light. The flashlight blinks several times a second and SweepLab compares frames with and without it. A point that lights up only with the flash is worth a look; a lamp or an LED stays lit either way.
  • Steady light if flashing lights affect you or anyone in the room. It works the same way, with a little less certainty. SweepLab asks before the first strobe.
  • Sweep coaching. A lens only flashes back from roughly in front of it and within about 2–3 m, so the app tells you when you're moving too fast and reminds you to sweep from where a camera would be looking: the bed, the shower, the desk.
  • Shiny screws, glass edges and water drops glint too, so every ring is a spot to check by hand, never a verdict.

04Infrared check

Night-vision lights your eyes can't see

Many cameras light a dark room with infrared LEDs. Some iPhone cameras can see that light; SweepLab starts with the front camera and helps you find one that works.

  • The TV-remote test. Hold a classic infrared TV remote 10–30 cm from the camera and press any button. If the camera sees the remote's light, it can see infrared. If not, SweepLab suggests trying another camera.
  • Pan slowly with the lights off. Violet rings mark small, steady lights. If you can't see one with your own eyes, it may be infrared.
  • Highlights on paper, walls and shiny boxes are ignored: a light has to be a pinpoint on its own. The same light seen again as you pan counts once.
  • Tap the moon to dim the screen so it doesn't light the room. Rings still appear, and you feel a tap.

If none of your iPhone's cameras sees the remote, they filter infrared too strongly for this check; the lens scan is your backstop.

05Wi-Fi radar · SweepLab Puck

A small listener for what uploads like a camera

A camera streaming video uploads steadily, all the time. The optional SweepLab Puck sits in the room and listens to every Wi-Fi device in range, even on networks you can't join, and SweepLab ranks them by how much they upload like that.

  • The signal ladder. Rows show how strong each device's signal is: Very close, In the room, Nearby, Far, or Signal unknown when it's only heard through the router. Further right means it uploads more like a camera.
  • Scores from 0 to 100. 50 or more is Worth a look. Open a device and carry the puck around the room: its signal number climbs as you get close.
  • It only listens. The puck never joins a Wi-Fi network and never transmits on Wi-Fi. It counts how much each device sends, never what. Addresses are scrambled on the puck; only the maker prefix reaches your iPhone.
  • A high score means "uploads steadily", not "is a camera". A TV streaming, a laptop backing up or a phone on a video call look the same, which is what the motion test is for.

The puck is optional and not on sale yet. Read about the puck.

06The motion test

Does anything react to you?

A camera's upload shrinks when the room it watches goes empty and still. A TV's doesn't care. The app coaches you through it and the puck measures every device both ways.

  1. Walk around the room for 15 seconds. Move like you normally would: walk, wave, sit down and stand up.
  2. Step out and close the door. You have 8 seconds. Take the phone or leave it on a table; either works.
  3. Stay outside for 15 seconds while the puck measures the empty room. Your phone buzzes when you can come back in.

Devices whose upload dropped a lot are marked reacted to you and go to the top of your results. Nothing reacting lowers the odds; it can't rule a camera out.

07Results

Things to look at, most urgent first

Results never say "camera found". They say what was seen, where, and what to do about it.

  • Covered so far lists which checks ran and what each found, so a quiet result is never mistaken for a clear room.
  • Every lens or infrared spot keeps a snapshot of where it was. Go and look with your own flashlight, then tap I looked: not a camera.
  • Still to look at by hand: the usual hiding places you haven't ticked off yet.
  • If you find a camera: don't touch it, photograph it where it is, report it to the booking platform or the front desk and to the local police, and leave if you feel unsafe. More in the guide.

08Honest limits

What it can and can't do

None of these checks can promise a room is clear. Each catches some cameras and misses others, so SweepLab runs all four and sends you to look by hand.

A score

Is not a verdict

A high Wi-Fi score means "uploads steadily", not "is a camera". A TV streaming, a laptop backing up or a phone on a video call look the same. The motion test is how to tell them apart.

The light checks

Need the right angle

A lens only flashes back when you're nearly in line with it and within about 2–3 m. Pinholes behind dark glass can be missed. A camera that's switched off only shows up in the lens scan.

The puck

Can't hear everything

It can miss cameras that record to a memory card, use a cellular SIM or are wired, cameras whose router is out of its range (about −80 dBm), and uploads hidden inside Wi-Fi 6 group transmissions. Devices on fast 5 GHz networks may show as "signal unknown".

A clear result

Isn't proof

Nothing flagged lowers the odds; it doesn't mean a room has no cameras. That's why the last step is always looking by hand. The full list

10FAQ

Common questions

Will SweepLab find every hidden camera?

No, and nothing can promise that. SweepLab gives you four ways to check and a list of things to look at. Cameras that are switched off, out of the flashlight's reach, wired, on a cellular SIM or recording to a memory card can be missed by some or all of the checks. A clear result lowers the odds; it isn't proof.

Do I need the puck?

No. The lens scan, the infrared check and looking by hand need only your iPhone. The puck adds the Wi-Fi radar and the motion test. In the room check, tap I don't have a puck, skip.

Does the puck connect to the rental's Wi-Fi or read anyone's traffic?

No. It never joins a network, never transmits on Wi-Fi and never reads what's inside the traffic it hears. It notes how much each device sends and how steadily, scrambles each device's address on the spot, and sends your iPhone only that summary over Bluetooth. See how the puck handles data.

Why does my TV show up as "worth a look"?

Because it uploads steadily, like a camera streaming video. Laptops backing up and phones on video calls do too. Tap This is mine to dim your own devices for this scan, and use the motion test: a camera's upload drops when the room empties; a TV's doesn't.

Which iPhones work?

Any iPhone with iOS 18 or later. Whether the infrared check works depends on your iPhone's cameras: the TV-remote test tells you. If none of them sees infrared, rely on the lens scan.

Is anything uploaded or saved?

No. There's no account and the app makes no internet connections. Camera frames are analysed on the phone as you scan; spots and Wi-Fi results live in memory and are gone when you close the app. See the privacy policy.
