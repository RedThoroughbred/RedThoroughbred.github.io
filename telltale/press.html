Press kit

Everything you need to write about Telltale. Use any of it, and ask for anything that's missing.

In one sentence

Telltale walks you through checking a rental or hotel room for hidden cameras and trackers in under ten minutes, with only your iPhone, and shows you where to look instead of pretending a room is clear.

The pitch

Hidden cameras in rentals are a real worry, and the App Store is full of "detector" apps that show a scary number and charge every week to explain it. Telltale is the opposite: free, with no subscription, no ads and nothing collected. It runs five checks that use what an iPhone can really sense, says what it saw and why, and is honest about what it can't see.

It does a few things no other camera-check app we know of does: a lights-off test that rings the lights that switch on after you turn the room light off (night-vision cameras turn their infrared on in the dark), flash-only rings that mark points lighting up only when the flashlight pulses, a swept strip that shows which directions you've covered, and a Wi-Fi check that only listens and never probes the host's devices.

Key facts

  • Name: Telltale (on the App Store: Telltale: Hidden Camera Check)
  • Platform: iPhone, iOS 18 or later
  • Price: free. No subscription, no in-app purchases, no ads.
  • Privacy: no account; nothing is uploaded; the App Store label is "Data Not Collected".
  • Checks: lens glint scan, infrared check with a TV-remote self-test, lights-off test, listen-only network check, Bluetooth tracker check, a by-hand checklist; results as one ranked list and a PDF report.
  • Developer: Seth Egger, an independent developer in Walton, Kentucky. Also makes WattBench, Repot, Wildcat Mesh and RedSynth Pad.
  • Availability: coming soon to the App Store.

How it works

  • Lens glint scan: a camera lens bounces light straight back. The flashlight pulses while you sweep, and Telltale rings points that light up only with the flash.
  • Infrared check and lights-off test: many cameras light a dark room with infrared LEDs. Some iPhone cameras can see them; a TV remote shows which. Then prop the phone up and switch the light off: Telltale rings what came on afterwards.
  • Network check: listens for about 25 seconds to what devices on the rental's Wi-Fi announce about themselves, and points out the camera-like ones. It never connects to them.
  • Tracker check: listens for Bluetooth item trackers (Tile, SmartTag, Chipolo, Pebblebee, Find Hub tags) that stay close as you move. It can't hear AirTags; iOS covers those itself.

Words to use, and not

  • Say: "checks a room", "shows you where to look", "points that light up only with the flash", "lights that came on in the dark", "listens to the Wi-Fi".
  • Please don't say: "detects cameras", "finds every camera", "guarantees a room is safe" or "camera detector". No app can promise that, and Telltale never claims to. A ring is a place to look, not a verdict.
  • It's Telltale, one word, capital T.
