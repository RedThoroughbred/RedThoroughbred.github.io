01Start here

What Telltale is and what you need

Telltale helps you check a room you're staying in, such as a rental, a hotel room or a short stay, for hidden cameras and trackers. It doesn't decide whether something is a camera. It points you at the spots and devices worth a closer look, and you go and look.

  • An iPhone with iOS 18 or later. The lens and infrared checks use its flashlight and cameras.
  • A dark room: lights off, curtains closed. The light checks work far better in the dark.
  • For the infrared check's self-test, a TV remote of the classic infrared kind (many streaming remotes use Bluetooth instead).
  • For the network check, the rental's Wi-Fi, the network the host gave you.
  • No account. Nothing leaves your iPhone.
  1. Turn the lights off and close the curtains.
  2. Open Telltale and tap Start the room check.
  3. Follow each step. Skip any you can't do.
  4. Go through Look at these, in order with your own flashlight.

02Home

Check this room

Five checks, about eight minutes

The home screen leads with the check ring: one coloured segment for each check, filling in as each one runs. A segment glows when its check is done, gets an amber dot when it flagged something, and shows dashes when you skipped it. Under the title, a line counts the checks and minutes, or how many have run and how many things there are to look at.

The main button reads Start the room check, then Continue the room check, then See what to look at once every step is done. New room forgets this room and starts over.

Below it, Or run one check lists each check on its own row with its state. At the bottom: What this can and can't find.

03The room check

Five checks, in order

What to do, why it works, skip, redo

The guided check shows the ring at the top and opens the current step with What to do (three or four things, in order) and a Why this works line you can open. One button at the bottom starts the step. Finished steps fold up to what they found, such as nothing flagged or 2 spots to check, with a Redo button.

1 · Lens glint scan
About 2 minutes. Sweep the room with the flashlight. See Lens glint scan.
2 · Infrared check
About 1 minute, plus the lights-off test. See Infrared check and Lights-off test.
3 · Network check
About 1 minute on the rental's Wi-Fi. See Network check.
4 · Tracker check
About 2 minutes with your bags. See Tracker check.
5 · Look by hand
About 2 minutes. Tick off the usual hiding places, then tap I've looked: show the results. See Look by hand.

Skip this check moves on without running a step; the results show it as skipped, so a quiet result is never mistaken for a full check. Start over clears the spots found so far and the checklist.

04Lens glint scan

Sweep with the flashlight

Strobe or steady light, sweep coaching, the swept strip, rings

A camera lens bounces light straight back toward where it came from. In a dark room, the flashlight makes a lens show up as a tiny, sharp, bright point, even through a pinhole. The bounce only comes back when the phone is roughly in front of the lens (inside the camera's own view) and within about 2–3 m, which is as far as the iPhone's flashlight reaches.

Strobe or steady

The first time, Telltale explains that the scan switches the flashlight on and off several times a second, and asks you to choose:

  • Use flashing light: Telltale compares frames with and without the flash. A point that lights up only with the flash gets a ring; a lamp or an LED stays lit either way. This makes lens reflections much easier to tell apart from shiny surfaces.
  • Use steady light: the flashlight stays on. It works the same way, with a little less certainty.

Flashing light. If flashing lights affect you or anyone in the room, choose Use steady light.

Doing the scan

  1. Turn the lights off and close the curtains. Hold the phone at eye level.
  2. If Telltale shows Measuring the flashlight, point at a wall or furniture 0.5–2 m away and hold still for two seconds.
  3. Stand where a camera would want to look: the bed, the shower, the desk.
  4. Sweep slowly across every wall, shelf and object, about a hand-width a second, within 2–3 m of what you're checking. Then do it again from a second spot.
  5. Tap Done.

The pill at the top coaches your pace: Sweep slowly, Good pace, or Slow down when you're moving too fast for a lens to flash back.

The swept strip

Swept around you fills in as you turn and tilt: turn left and the cells on the strip's left fill; tilt up and the top row fills. It tells you which direction to try next, and says All the way round when you've covered the room.

What the rings mean

A ring marks each spot. Tap a ring to zoom in on it. The bottom bar counts Spots found in this scan and how many are In view.

Lit up only with the flash
Strobe light. A small point that lit up only with the flash: could be a camera lens. Look closely at that spot from where you stood, or photograph it.
Bright pinpoint that stayed put
Steady light. A small, sharp point that stayed in place while you moved: could be a lens, a small light or a shiny screw. Switch your flashlight off: a lens goes dark, an LED does not.

Shiny screws, glass edges, glossy plastic, water drops and tiny indicator lights glint too. A ring is a spot to check by hand, not proof of a camera.

05Infrared check

Look for night-vision lights

The TV-remote test, then a slow pan

Many cameras light a dark room with infrared LEDs your eyes can't see. Some iPhone cameras can see them, as small purple or white dots, and some can't, because of the filter in front of the sensor. Front and rear cameras filter differently, which is why Telltale tests them.

The TV-remote test

The first time, the check starts with a quick self-test (run it again any time with Remote test):

  1. Get a TV remote ready. Hold it 10–30 cm from the camera being tested, pointing at it. Don't press yet.
  2. Press and hold any button now. Use a classic infrared remote: many streaming remotes use Bluetooth instead.
  3. If Telltale says This camera sees infrared, tap Start the IR check. If it says This camera didn't see the remote, try the next camera, or Test again.

Telltale remembers the answer for each camera and uses one that sees infrared.

If none of your iPhone's cameras sees the remote, they filter infrared too strongly for this check. Rely on the lens glint scan instead, or try another remote.

The check

  1. Keep the lights off.
  2. Pan slowly around the room. With the front camera, turn the screen toward the room.
  3. Violet rings mark small, steady lights. If you can't see one with your own eyes, it may be infrared.
  4. Tap Done, or go on to the lights-off test.

Only pinpoints alone in the dark count: highlights on paper, a wall or a shiny box are ignored, and the same light seen again as you pan counts once. Tap the moon at the top to dim the screen so it doesn't light the room; rings still appear and you feel a tap.

06Lights-off test

See what switches on in the dark

Phone still, light off, 15 seconds

A night-vision camera has a light sensor. When the room goes dark, it switches its infrared LEDs on, usually within a few seconds. The lights-off test turns that into a before-and-after: it learns the lit room, waits for the dark, and rings only the lights that came on after it arrived.

  1. In the infrared check, tap Lights off (or Try the lights-off test). Use the camera that passed the TV-remote test.
  2. Prop the phone up facing one wall, with the room light still on. It has to stay still: lean it against something.
  3. When Telltale says Now switch the light off, switch it off. The screen goes dark too, so it doesn't light the room.
  4. For 15 seconds it watches. Watching the dark… counts what came on so far. You feel a tap when it's done.
  5. Do the same facing each wall that matters, especially the ones facing the bed and the shower.
Came on in the dark
A small light that switched on after the room went dark, with how many seconds it took. Go and look at what it's part of.
Already on: set aside
A standby LED that was only hidden by the room light shows up the moment the room goes dark. Those are set aside, not flagged.
Nothing came on in the dark
Nothing switched on facing this wall. That lowers the odds; it doesn't rule a camera out.
The phone moved
New lights only count if the phone stays still. Prop it against something and try again.
The room didn't get dark
Switch off every light, close the curtains and try again. A bright window or a lamp left on keeps the test from starting.

A night light on a timer, a charger's LED that lights in the dark, or a screen waking up can come on too. A ring is a place to look, not a verdict.

07Network check

What's on this Wi-Fi

About 25 seconds, listen only

Many Wi-Fi cameras announce themselves on the network they're joined to, by name or as a video service. Telltale listens to what the devices on the Wi-Fi you've joined say about themselves and points out the camera-like ones.

  1. Join the rental's Wi-Fi, the network the host gave you.
  2. Tap Start listening. The first time, iOS asks to let Telltale find devices on your local network: allow it.
  3. Keep Telltale open for about 25 seconds. Devices answer in their own time.
  4. Look at anything camera-like first: each one says why it's on the list.
Camera-like
The device's own announcements describe it as a camera or a video stream.
Maybe worth a look
Weaker hints, like a maker that also sells cameras or a smart display with a built-in camera.
Everything else
Printers, TVs, speakers and the like: nothing camera-like in what they announce.

Camera-like means the device is on this Wi-Fi, not that it's in your room: doorbells and outdoor cameras are common. Telltale only listens: it never connects to a device, logs in, opens a video stream or scans its ports. The list is gone when you close the app.

08Tracker check

Trackers that stay close to you

About 75 seconds, listen only

Item trackers like Tile, Samsung SmartTag, Chipolo, Pebblebee and Google Find Hub tags send short Bluetooth signals all the time. Telltale listens while you walk around with your things.

  1. Gather your bag, coat, keys and luggage, and turn Bluetooth on.
  2. Tap Start listening. The first time, iOS asks to let Telltale use Bluetooth; it's only used while this check runs.
  3. Follow the coach: first near your things, then walk around with them. Keep Telltale open; the scan stops if you leave the app.
  4. Look at anything worth a look first: each one says why it's listed and what to do.

A tag is worth a look when its signal says it has been away from its owner for a while, or when it stays close the whole time you move. Signal strength is a rough guide to distance: a bag, a body or a wall weakens it.

AirTags: iOS doesn't let apps hear them. Your iPhone warns you on its own when an unknown AirTag travels with you: make sure Settings → Notifications → Tracking Notifications is on, with Bluetooth and Location on.

If you find a tracker you don't know: photograph it where you found it and note where and when. Removing or switching it off may alert whoever placed it; if that could put you at risk, make a plan first. Contact the local police: the tag's serial number can help them find its owner. For stalking or abuse in the US, the National Domestic Violence Hotline is 1-800-799-7233, or text START to 88788.

09Look by hand

Nothing beats looking

The checklist

The usual hiding places are the ones with a view of the bed or the shower and a reason to have power. Look closely at each with your own flashlight and tick it off. Look for pinholes, odd lenses, and gadgets facing the bed or shower, and check anything the scans flagged, up close.

Bathroom
Start here. Shower area, towel hooks, the mirror frame and anything facing the toilet or shower.
Bedroom
Start here. Everything with a view of the bed: shelves, headboard, wall art, the TV.
Smoke detectors
The classic hiding place. Look for a pinhole facing the bed or shower.
Clocks and radios
Alarm clocks and speakers by the bed often face it directly.
Chargers and USB plugs
Wall chargers and power strips that seem out of place, or that face the room.
Vents and air purifiers
Grilles and slots high on the wall or ceiling.
TV area
The TV, set-top boxes, soundbars and anything on the shelf around them.

10Results and the PDF

What to look at

One ranked list from every check

Results gather everything the checks flagged into one list of things to look at by hand, most urgent first. They never say "camera found" or "all clear".

  • The ring with the number of things to look at in the middle, and how many checks ran.
  • Covered so far: each check and what it found (2 spots to check, nothing flagged, 4 of 7 places), or skipped / not run.
  • Look at these, in order: lens spots and infrared lights with a snapshot of where they were (tap it to see it larger, with a ring where the spot was) and a strength from 0 to 100; lights that came on in the dark; camera-like network devices; trackers worth a look. Show N weaker shows the rest.
  • When you've looked at a spot, tap I looked: not a camera. It moves to You checked these (tap again to undo).
  • Still to look at by hand: checklist places you haven't ticked, with Open checklist.

Keep a record

Save a report (PDF) makes a PDF of what was checked, what was flagged (with the snapshots) and the time. It stays on your phone unless you send it, for example to the host or the booking platform.

Results live in memory: closing Telltale or tapping New room clears them, so save the PDF first if you want to keep them.

11If you find a camera

What to do

  1. Don't touch it, unplug it or take it apart. Cover it with a towel if you need privacy right now.
  2. Photograph it where it is: close up, and from across the room so the position is clear. Save Telltale's PDF report.
  3. Report it to the booking platform or the hotel's front desk, and to the local police. Keep your booking details.
  4. If you feel unsafe, leave. Ask the platform or the hotel to move you; many booking platforms have a 24-hour safety line.

12What it can and can't find

Limits, honestly

None of these checks can promise a room is clear. Each catches some cameras and misses others, so run all five and look by hand. The same text is in the app under What this can and can't find.

What it can miss

  • A lens only flashes back when you're nearly in line with it, so sweep from several spots. Pinholes behind dark glass and anything beyond the flashlight's reach (about 2–3 m) can be missed.
  • A camera that is switched off only shows up in the lens scan. The infrared check and the lights-off test need it on, with night-vision lights.
  • Some iPhone cameras can't see infrared at all; the TV-remote test tells you.
  • The network check only hears devices on the Wi-Fi you joined that announce themselves. Cameras on another network, on a cellular SIM card, wired, recording to a memory card or staying quiet won't show up.
  • The tracker check can't hear AirTags, and many tags change their Bluetooth address often, so "stayed close" only covers one scan.
  • A clear result does not mean a room has no cameras.

What it can mistake for something

  • Lens scan: shiny screws, glass edges, glossy plastic, water drops and tiny indicator lights.
  • Infrared and lights-off: standby LEDs, night lights, chargers and screens.
  • Network check: a doorbell or outdoor camera on the same Wi-Fi, or a smart display with a built-in camera.
  • Tracker check: your own tags, or a tag in the room next door.

13Troubleshooting

When something doesn't work

Can't use the camera
Telltale needs the camera for the lens and infrared checks. Go to Settings → Telltale and turn on Camera.
Measuring the flashlight
Point at a wall or furniture 0.5–2 m away and hold still for two seconds.
Lots of rings
Shiny things glint. Slow down, sweep from a second spot, and check the strongest first. Switching your own flashlight off tells a lens (goes dark) from an LED (stays lit).
Remote not seen
Use a classic infrared remote and hold a button down 10–30 cm from the camera being tested. Try the next camera. If none sees it, rely on the lens scan.
The lights-off test won't start
The room has to get much darker than it was. Switch off every light and close the curtains; a bright window or a lamp left on keeps it waiting.
Not on Wi-Fi
The network check needs the rental's Wi-Fi. No Wi-Fi here? Skip the step.
Local Network access is off
Go to Settings → Telltale and turn on Local Network, then tap Listen again.
Bluetooth access is off
Go to Settings → Telltale and turn on Bluetooth, then listen again.
Results are gone
Results are kept only while the app is open. Run the checks again, and save the PDF next time.
